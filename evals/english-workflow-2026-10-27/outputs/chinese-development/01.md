## Summary
QuantCache evaluates cached, quantized features for batch-one sensor classification on three datasets. Matched configurations measure caching and quantization contributions to latency. The paper claims faster inference with at most .003 degradation in expected unseen-subject F1.

## Strengths
1. The controlled comparisons isolate caching and quantization: latency falls from 12.4 to 8.2 to 5.1 ms on the same CPU, including preprocessing and cache lookup.
2. Shared training budgets and seeds support comparison, and the reported 28 MB memory overhead makes the operating-point tradeoff explicit.

## Weaknesses
1. Section 2 selects each method's threshold by maximizing F1 on the full test labels and reports F1 on those same labels. This is confirmed evaluation leakage: the decision rule is adapted to the evaluation subjects, so these scores do not estimate the stated frozen pipeline's performance on unseen subjects. Select thresholds using validation data and evaluate the resulting frozen rules on independent held-out subjects.
2. The claim of at most .003 degradation in expected unseen-subject F1 exceeds the evidence. The observed mean differences are point estimates under the compromised protocol; the reported seed standard deviations explicitly do not quantify uncertainty over new subjects. After correcting evaluation, report the observed tradeoff and its supported uncertainty, or narrow the expectation-level claim.

## Overall Assessment
The matched timing results support a useful practical speed improvement within the stated scope. However, test-label threshold optimization undermines the central accuracy-preservation conclusion. I recommend substantial revision before acceptance, centered on independent accuracy evaluation and appropriately limited claims.
