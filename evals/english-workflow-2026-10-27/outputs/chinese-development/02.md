**Title**
A valid normalized bound, but an invalid raw-weight guarantee

**Paper Summary**
The note studies range preservation for nonnegative weighted sums of inputs in [0,1]. Theorem 1 claims this property for unrestricted raw weights. Proposition 2 establishes it for a separate normalized variant.

**Strengths**
1. The normalized construction has a valid argument: its nonnegative weights sum to one when the denominator is positive. Returning zero when all weights vanish also preserves the range.

**Weaknesses**
1. Theorem 1 is false. Set n = 2, w_1 = w_2 = 1, and x_1 = x_2 = 1; then S = 2. Nonnegativity does not imply that the weights sum to at most one, so the proof's decisive step fails. Proposition 2 does not repair this error because the note explicitly retains the raw-weight claim. For raw weights, the proved bound is [0, sum_i w_i]; a uniform [0,1] guarantee requires sum_i w_i <= 1 under the stated nonnegativity assumption.

**Justification of Recommendation**
I do not recommend acceptance as written: the sole formal guarantee for the raw variant is false. Correct the theorem's assumptions or restrict the advertised range guarantee to the normalized variant.
