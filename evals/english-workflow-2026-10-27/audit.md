**A02 — Significance language should not be read as proof of nonsignificance.** `inputs/S1/03.md:3` says the evidence “does not establish statistical significance”; `inputs/S3/03.md:3` says significance overstates the supplied evidence and claims. The excerpt supplies no significance claim, test, inferential assumptions, or chosen error threshold, so declining to endorse the draft's unconditional assertion is reasonable. However, the numerical evidence is not inherently incapable of producing significance: using the supplied five differences, a conventional paired t-test gives t = 7 with 4 degrees of freedom and a two-sided p ≈ 0.00219 if its assumptions hold; a two-sided exact sign test gives p = 0.0625. These calculations illustrate why the inference depends on the testing framework; they do not validate that framework or establish a population claim. S1's sentence is safest understood as “not established by the analysis reported here,” not “the effect cannot be statistically significant.” S3's wording allows the same interpretation. `inputs/S2/03.md:3` avoids the stronger negative phrasing and simply retains the authors' modest conclusion. This is a low-severity, conditional precision concern, with low confidence that it constitutes an actual error. No response asserts an effect on all domains.
